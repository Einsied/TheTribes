\chapter{Conflict}\label{ch:Conflict}

While the \hyperref[ch:World:Inhabitants:Animals]{animals} and the none
organized \hyperref[ch:World:Inhabitants:Sapients]{sapients} live in blissful
harmony the members of the \hyperref[ch:Tribes]{tribes} do not. This leads to
conflict, which often takes the form of combat.

\section{Direct combat}\label{ch:Conflict:Combat}

Direct combat refers to a fight between multiple
\hyperref[ch:World:Inhabitants] of the world. While members of the
\hyperref[ch:Tribes]{tribes} usually use \glspl{Weapon} to do harm to each
other \hyperref[ch:World:Inhabitants:Animals]{animals} or
\hyperref[ch:World:Inhabitants:Livestock]{livestock} use their bodies, hooves,
claws and teeth.

There is more than one way to do harm and the results are different. The
methods to \hyperref[ch:Goods:Armory:Armor]{avoid harm} or recover from it are
also different. In general all harm gets slowly undone, but some can be cured
faster with special supplies. Once there is more harm done than can be absorbed
the \emph{final effect}, like death, occurs. Here is a short overview:

\begin{longtable}{llll}
	\toprule
	Harm     & Recovery speed & Recovery \hyperref[ch:Goods]{goods} & Final Effect                                                                                                                     \\
	\midrule
	Physical & Lowest         & \Gls{Medicine}                      & Death                                                                                                                            \\
	Morale   & Faster         & \Gls{Drink}                         & Panic\footnote{The affected creatures flees the battle if it can or surrenders otherwise (see \autoref{ch:Conflict:Surrender}).} \\
	Stun     & Medium         & None                                & Loss of consciousness\footnote{This permits the creatures opponent to capture or kill it.}                                       \\
	\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Harm can also be distinguished by the way it is distributed. A blade strikes
one opponent directly, an arrow travels to its target and a comrade being split
in two strikes fear in all around.

\begin{longtable}{ll}
	\toprule
	Category       & Description                                             \\
	\midrule
	Distribution   & If the harm only affects a single target or an area.    \\
	Delivery       & If the harm is applied directly or via an projectile.   \\
	Discrimination & If the harm discriminates between opponents and allies. \\
	\bottomrule
\end{longtable}


\section{Surrender}\label{ch:Conflict:Surrender}

If a soldiers morale breaks and they feels
they are unable to flee they may decide to surrender instead.
This usually occurs if they are surrounded by enemy soldiers in melee.
They will raise their arms and hope for an opportunity to flee.
A soldier of an opposing side can claim a surrendering soldier as a convert.
In exchange for a chance of survival they turn into a convert.
Since converts do no labor, before they are full members of their new tribes,
they are forced to continue the fight sometimes.
If they are fighting to the death their morale no longer matters.
If the converts are accepted the next steps depend on the tribe.

The \gls{Vikings} send their converts to their sanctuaries,
where they mediate to appreciate the gift of life and nature as whole.
During this meditation their bodies are nourished by the spirits in the sanctuaries.
The duration of the meditation depends on the size of the sanctuary
and the number of converts already meditating there.
Fewer converts and bigger sanctuaries speed up conversion,
it is therefore wise to split up the converts to multiple sanctuaries.

The \gls{Nomads} will not accept cowards or weaklings within their hordes.
Therefore converts are forced to fight each other,
the stronger on joining the \gls{Nomads} the weaker one his dead ancestors.
As there is no guarantee that there is an equal number of converts after battle,
a lone convert may be seen following the horde hoping or fearing for the battle that will
enable their duel.

To joint the \gls{Egyptians} the converts walk to the next necropolis.
There they swear fealty to the King of the afterlife and the royal family.
before they are judged bz a priest of the mortuary cult.
If they are found wanting their flesh turns to dust leaving all their mortal possessions behind
the lucky few that get accepted receive their magical talisman.

Unlucky are those that are offered the chance to help the \gls{Aztecs} save the world.
Some of them may hope for a serving of bean paste or another chance on the battlefield,
but the sun is weak and needs nourishment,
so the converts are brought to the serpents stable and fed to the sky serpents there.

Everyone who wishes to be a subject of the \gls{Japanese} gains a chance to find their place.
They are brought to the examination office to study there among the unchosen,
afterwards they are normal subjects of the \gls{Japanese}.

Surrendering to the legions of the \gls{Romans}, means facing a stark choice.
Forsake and curse your gods now or be executed on the spot.
Many fail to do the former and their blood is spilled to ensure the freedom of the \gls{Romans}.

\section{Siege warfare}\label{ch:Conflict:Sieges}

Siege-warfare refers to fight between members of the
\hyperref[ch:Tribes]{tribes} and fortifications often manned by members of
other \hyperref[ch:Tribes]{tribes}. Here we will discuss how the different kind
of fortifications influence \hyperref[ch:Conflict:Combat]{combat} and how they
can be destroyed or their advantages neutralized by siege-engines.
